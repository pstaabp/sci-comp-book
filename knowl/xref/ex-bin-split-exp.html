Exercise 24.5.

Use binary splitting to find the first four terms of the series for \(e^x\) when \(x=1\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
In this case, note that the series:
\begin{equation*} e = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k!} \end{equation*}
and since we need to have a series of the form:
\begin{equation*} S_k = S_{k-1} \cdot \frac{P_k}{Q_k} \end{equation*}
this holds with \(P_k=1\) for all \(k\) and \(Q_k = k\) when \(k \geq 1\) and \(Q_0=1\text{.}\)
🔗
The first four terms of the leaves then are
\begin{align*} P_0 \amp = 1 \amp Q_0 \amp = 1 \amp T_0 \amp = 1\\ P_1 \amp = 1 \amp Q_1 \amp = 1 \amp T_1 \amp = 1\\ P_2 \amp = 1 \amp Q_2 \amp = 2 \amp T_2 \amp = 1\\ P_3 \amp = 1 \amp Q_3 \amp = 3 \amp T_3 \amp = 1 \end{align*}
🔗
For the next stage:
\begin{align*} P_{0..2} \amp = P_0 P_1 = 1 \amp Q_{0..2} \amp = Q_0 Q_1 = 1 \amp T_{0..2} = \amp T_0 Q_1 + P_0 T_1 = 2 \\ P_{2..4} \amp = P_2 P_3 = 1 \amp Q_{2..4} \amp = Q_2 Q_3 = 6 \amp T_{2..4} = \amp T_2 Q_3 + P_2 T_3 = 4 \end{align*}
🔗
and the last stage is
\begin{align*} P_{0..4} \amp = P_{0..2} P_{2..4} = 1 \amp Q_{0..4} = \amp Q_{0..2} Q_{2..4} = 6 \\ T_{0..4} \amp = T_{0..2} Q_{2..4} + P_{0..2} T_{2..4} = 2(6) + (1)(4) = 16 \end{align*}
and the resulting fraction is
\begin{equation*} \frac{T_{0..4}}{Q_{0..4}} = \frac{16}{6} = \frac{8}{3} \end{equation*}
and this is the same as the first four terms or
\begin{equation*} 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{16}{6} = \frac{8}{3} \end{equation*}
